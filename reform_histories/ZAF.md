## (b) Turning points

### 1. 2003–2012: from 45th to 101st

**Mechanisms.** South Africa's early position rested on post-1994 administrative reform rather than on a digital programme. The 1997 Batho Pele White Paper set service standards and access principles. SITA (1999) pooled IT capacity, and DPSA's 2001 IT policy framework and MIOS gave e-government a policy home (DTPS2017). A few strong agency systems followed: SARS eFiling, eNaTIS and the Batho Pele Gateway portal (2004). These were enough for the content-heavy, portal-based surveys of 2003–2005. The decline came when the survey began to reward integrated transactional services. By the government's own account, the programme "has not been directed and managed in a collaborative manner". It had no dedicated budget, the roles of the OGCIO, SITA and departmental GITOs were unclear, and most systems "were not designed to share information across departments" (DTPS2017, pp. 15–16). The mandate also moved between DPSA, DTPS and SITA (DTPS2017). Peer-reviewed studies describe the same pattern. Mutula and Mostert (2010) found enabling policies and infrastructure that "have not been effectively leveraged" for service delivery. Maumbe, Owei and Alexander (2008) questioned whether the Western Cape's Cape Gateway portal matched local socio-economic needs. Municipal studies point to weak governance, leadership, skills and funding (Mawela, Ochara & Twinomurinzi, 2017).

**Limits.** EGDI rank is relative. Between 2003 and 2012 many countries entered or improved, and the index changed method (2008 and 2010 edition redesigns), so part of the fall reflects others moving ahead. No source we verified measures South African service output falling in absolute terms. The diagnosis of fragmentation comes from a 2017 government document written after the fact, and its wording may be shaped by the case for a new strategy.

### 2. 2012–2024: recovery to 40th (Africa #1)

**Mechanisms.** The recovery came mainly from a few high-volume identity and revenue services, and from moving delivery out of overloaded offices. The Smart ID card (2013) and eHomeAffairs with bank-branch kiosks (2016) addressed a capacity limit the Presidency stated openly. More than 38 million green ID books had to be replaced through 407 Home Affairs offices "not designed for this capacity" (PRES2016). SARS eFiling reached mass use, which strengthened the online-service component. The 2017 Strategy recognised fragmentation for the first time. It proposed a central portal, a monitoring and evaluation strategy with agreed indicators, and a new role for SITA as a "Digital Transformation Entity" (DTPS2017, pp. 16, 34). The trust side was completed when POPIA took effect in 2020–2021 (PRES2020; Staunton et al., 2020). COVID-19 forced online social-relief channels into place (SRD grant, TERS), which the 2025 Roadmap credits as a catalyst (RSA2025).

Researchers also began to measure service quality against the Batho Pele principles: Kaisara and Pather (2011) built a Batho Pele-aligned e-service quality instrument. That is an early, Batho Pele-specific contribution to the measurement dimension.

**Limits.** Adoption stayed uneven. Among taxpayers who still filed manually, the main barrier to eFiling was lack of access to computers and the internet (Jankeeparsad, Jankeeparsad & Nienaber, 2017). Municipal e-government strategy processes were weakened by group dynamics and resource gaps (Osah & Khene, 2018). The contested Gauteng e-toll project shows how weak public participation can erode trust in ICT-based public projects (Mawela, Ochara & Twinomurinzi, 2016). In 2022 departments still named weak inter-departmental governance, legacy integration, insufficient funding and duplicated systems as the main obstacles (Maremi, Thulare & Herselman, 2022). The 2017 Strategy's M&E strategy and central portal cannot be confirmed as delivered from the sources we checked. The 2018–2020 dip (68th to 78th) shows the recovery was not steady. The jump from 65th (2022) to 40th (2024) coincides with the TII revision, so part of it may be an artefact of the index. We could not separate the two effects.

### 3. 2024–2026: Africa #1 at 40th, then 52nd

**Mechanisms.** Governance changed in 2025. A Presidency-led Inter-Departmental Working Group, a ministerial committee and a Digital Service Unit now sit inside Operation Vulindlela, the government's structural-reform programme. They are organised around four DPI building blocks: digital ID, data exchange, payments, and one channel (RSA2025). This is the first arrangement that places authority above line departments. It answers the 2017 diagnosis directly. Delivery has started: the 2026 bank model uses an API gateway rather than miniature offices inside banks (DHA2026), and 753 branches were live by November 2026 (SANEWS2026).

**Limits.** In 2026 most Roadmap components were still prototypes. The digital ID launched as a prototype on 1 October 2026, about nine months after the Roadmap's Q4 2025 target, and MyMzansi was still in a test environment (OV2026; RSA2025). The drop to 52nd therefore fits a reading in which the 2026 survey measured services that were not yet public, while other countries moved faster. This is an inference; we did not verify it against the 2026 survey's component scores.

## (c) What is transferable to Libya, and what depends on conditions Libya lacks

**Transferable.**
- A people-first service charter (Batho Pele) that defines standards and redress before any technology is chosen.
- Starting with one or two high-volume, revenue- or identity-linked services and building them well (SARS eFiling, Smart ID).
- Using existing private networks, especially bank branches, as assisted channels when state offices lack capacity. The 2026 version connects them through an API instead of building offices inside banks.
- Putting coordination authority at the centre of government with a small technical unit, rather than in a line ministry or the state IT company.
- Passing a data-protection law and setting up a regulator before linking registers.

**Dependent on conditions Libya lacks.**
- One national government with uncontested authority and a stable budget process. South Africa still took about 25 years to centralise coordination.
- A large, well-capitalised and nationally networked banking sector willing to share costs.
- A tax administration with the autonomy and capacity of SARS.
- A national population register already in use for the Smart ID.
- An independent information regulator and courts able to enforce POPIA.
- A reform programme (Operation Vulindlela) with presidential and Treasury backing.

## (e) Not verified

- **Exact start of SARS eFiling in 2001.** The only source is a 2012 SAnews feature (outsourced, VAT only). We found no SARS primary document. One SAnews piece instead dates the introduction of individual eFiling to 2007; the SARS release of 1 June 2006 contradicts that.
- **SITA Act 88 of 1998: assent and gazette details.** Assent 12 Oct. 1998 and GG 19371 of 16 Oct. 1998 appeared in a search summary, but the gov.za PDF (a88-98.pdf) is a scanned image and could not be read. The Act number and the April 1999 establishment are verified (SITA2026; DTPS2017).
- **Batho Pele White Paper: gazette details.** GG 18340, Notice 1459, 1 Oct. 1997 is not shown on the loaded gov.za page.
- **DHA Smart ID statement.** The DHA page was not loaded. The 18 July 2013 date rests on its title and on GOVZA2013 (title and date only).
- **PC4IR report: gazette details.** GG 43834 of 23 Oct. 2020 and the eight recommendations come from search summaries only. The official pages loaded confirm only the May 2019 start and the 6 Aug. 2020 handover.
- **Delivery of the 2017 Strategy's outputs.** Whether the central portal, the M&E strategy and SITA's "Digital Transformation Entity" role were delivered is not confirmed by any source we checked.
- **Index effects.** We could not quantify how much of the 2024 jump comes from the TII revision, or confirm which components explain the 2026 fall.
- **Cabinet approval of the 2025 Roadmap.** March 2025 approval and May 2025 launch come from search summaries. The PDF confirms Cabinet endorsement but gives no date.
